# Sampling workflow

**Aliases:** sampler, sample, one-shot, loop, DJ tag, riser, sweep, vocal
punctuation, percussion layer, Sample Tease

Sampling adds short vocals, drums, effects, or musical fragments above the main
tracks. Learn this after cues, fades, and loops so samples decorate a stable mix
rather than conceal timing problems.

## Categories

- One-shots: horns, impacts, vocal shouts, and effects.
- Drum hits: kicks, snares, and percussion.
- Loops: repeating rhythmic or musical material.
- Tags: DJ name or signature sounds.
- Atmosphere: risers, sweeps, and transition effects.

## Preparation

1. Use audio you have the right to perform, distribute, or record.
2. Trim silence and normalize conservatively outside a live set.
3. Load samples into a saved Mixxx sampler bank and label each pad clearly.
4. Decide whether each sample is one-shot, gate, or loop mode.
5. Set its gain below the main track, then audition through headphones and the actual master path.
6. For melodic samples, match or complement the playing key.

## Moves

- **Vocal Punctuation:** trigger one short vocal immediately before a drop or transition; avoid competing vocals.
- **Percussion Layer:** add percussion to a sparse section and remove it before the arrangement becomes busy.
- **Transition Sweep:** start a riser in the final phrase and land the fade/drop at its endpoint.
- **Sample Tease:** play a recognizable fragment of Track B before introducing the full song.

On the Starlight, verify the active pad mode before performing: the same four
physical pads may represent hot cues, loops, or samples depending on mapping.
