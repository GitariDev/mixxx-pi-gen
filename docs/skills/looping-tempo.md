# Looping and tempo matching

**Aliases:** loop, beat loop, loop roll, tempo match, beatmatch, drift, jog,
Extended Runway, Outro Rescue, Tempo Bridge

Loops manipulate arrangement time. They can extend a short mixable intro or
outro, hold a transition area, or build tension. A loop must be grid- and
phrase-aware to avoid sounding accidental.

## Loop sizes

- **16 beats:** extend an intro/outro with more musical breathing room.
- **8 beats:** safest general-purpose loop and default starting point.
- **4 beats:** tighter performance timing.
- **1-2 beats:** tension/effect; not the beginner default.

Confirm the beatgrid, enable Quantize, start on the first beat of a bar or
phrase, and prefer stable instrumental material. Avoid vocals, fills, or crash
cymbals that make repetition obvious.

## Manual tempo workflow

1. Read or estimate both BPMs and adjust Track B's tempo to Track A.
2. Cue B's downbeat and launch on the correct phrase.
3. Listen in headphones for drifting kicks, claps, or hi-hats.
4. Nudge the jog wheel until beats align.
5. If drift continues, make a small tempo correction and listen again.

Sync can align BPM and phase when analysis is correct, but it cannot repair a
bad grid or a launch on the wrong musical phrase.

## Moves

- **Extended Runway:** loop B's intro, match and fade it in, then release when A reaches its exit.
- **Outro Rescue:** loop A's outro for 8 or 16 beats while cueing and introducing B.
- **Loop Roll Build:** shorten 8 to 4 to 2 to 1 beat, then release on the drop. Use sparingly.
- **Tempo Bridge:** loop a stable section, change tempo gradually, then phrase-match into the new BPM range. Prefer an intermediate track over extreme stretching.
