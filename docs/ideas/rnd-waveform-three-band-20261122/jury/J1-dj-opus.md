## 2. Pairwise (mid-set use)
- **A vs B: A.** A's kicks show as notches in the blue and the look is pure rekordbox. B is today's brick painted in three colours, with fixed-looking rims that tell me nothing.
- **A vs C: C.** In C the bass is never buried under the highs, and the bass entry jumps out. In A the white sits on top of the bass core and the mids mostly vanish.
- **A vs D: D.** When I'm lining up a swap I need to know where the bass is and where the hats are. D shows both separately with no guessing. A is more familiar but hides the mids.
- **B vs C: C.** C gives me a bass level I can read on its own. B gives me proportions inside the old outline, and the loud master is still a slab.
- **B vs D: D.** D actually separates the bands. B only colours the outline.
- **C vs D: D, narrowly.** Both keep the bass in the clear. D also untangles mids from highs and puts hat/snare spikes on the grid. C keeps more of a waveform shape and is a closer second than the totals suggest.

## 3. Against today
- **A: better.** You can read bass and highs at a glance, the kicks show, and the colours are calmer. Its only loss is size on quiet tracks.
- **B: better, but only slightly.** It adds a bass core to the same brick. Kicks are still lost on loud masters.
- **C: better.** It gives the clearest single "where's the bass" cue of the set.
- **D: better.** It gives the most information per column. It loses the familiar shape and the quiet-track size.

**What I would change in D:**
- Rebalance the lanes. Make bass about 50 % of the row height and mids and highs about 25 % each, because bass is what I mix on.
- Give the highs and mids lanes their own gain, about 2× today, or a per-track normaliser, so a quiet master like deck 2 doesn't flatline to a 1 px line.
- Draw the bass lane from peak values, not averages, so each kick shows as a tooth instead of the current flat comb.
- Add a 1 px dark-grey floor line under each lane so the empty lanes still read as "silent" rather than "missing".

If those fixes can't bring quiet masters up, C is the safer pick: it keeps a single waveform body and its bass is just as clear.
