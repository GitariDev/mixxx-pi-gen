Part 1 · Wire the 30 mm fan to the header

Needle or safety pinHeat-shrink or electrical tapeSingle-pin housings preferredSuitable heat source if using heat-shrink
  1. 1

    Shut down and unplug

    Run sudo poweroff. Wait until the green LED stops flashing, then disconnect USB-C and any powered hub or separate display supply. Leave the screen connector in place.

    Check: no lights on the board.

  2. 2

    Find the locking tabs

    Turn the fan's black plug until you see a small window over each pin. Identify what retains the contact: a flexible plastic latch on many Dupont housings, or a metal locking tang on others. Do not assume which type you have.

    Check: locate the latch before inserting the needle; stop if you cannot identify it.

  3. 3

    Release each pin

    Push the wire slightly inward first. For a plastic latch, lift it just enough to free the contact. For a metal tang, depress it through the window. Gently withdraw the wire toward the cable end. Do one contact at a time; never force it.

    Check: both pins are out and their tabs are still there.

  4. 4

    Insulate each pin

    Prefer separate 1-pin housings that lock around each contact. For a temporary setup, sleeve each contact individually with heat-shrink covering all outer metal while leaving the socket opening clear. Apply heat away from the Pi and cardboard. Tape alone is not a durable gig connector; if insulation or retention is doubtful, wait for jumpers.

    Check: no metal shows except the opening.

  5. 5

    Find your pins

    Example shown: screen on pins 4 + 6; fan red on pin 2, black on pin 14. Actual screen pins remain unconfirmed. Identify pin 1 by its square solder pad at the end farthest from the USB ports; count physical pins, not GPIO numbers. Leave the screen connector untouched.

    Check: if screen red occupies pin 2, use pin 4 for fan red. If neither 5 V pin is accessible, stop and use an adapter.

  6. 6

    Push on, then tug

    With all power disconnected, seat each insulated socket on its verified pin. Secure the cable to a nearby support so a tug cannot pull the contacts off. Check retention gently; never force a loose or damaged contact.

    Check: nothing bare touches a neighbouring pin.

  7. 7

    Power on and check

    Secure the fan clear of the board and loose wires before restoring power. Check that it starts. Use its airflow arrow or feel the flow; the label side is only a usual guide. Disconnect power again before mounting or adjusting it.

    Check: if it doesn't spin, power off and check red is on 5 V and black on pin 14.

Part 2 · Later: mount the 30 mm fan over the heatsink

M2.5 male-female standoff kitSmall screwdriverRuler
  1. 8

    Measure the stack

    Measure the tallest heatsink (h). Measure the fan thickness too (7 mm is an assumption). The support height must allow the heatsink height + 5–10 mm clear air gap + any fan thickness below the lid; account for the actual bracket.

    Check: h + 15 mm is only an example; confirm clearance and matching lid holes before buying.

  2. 9

    Fit standoffs and the lid

    When ready to change the mount, disconnect all power. If the lid holes and existing threads match, swap the Pi's screws for standoffs one at a time, so the Pi stays on the screen. Screw the lid on top with the fan over the SoC, offset about 5 mm. The fan's label faces the board.

    Check: the fan clears the heatsinks, and the hub on the floor below.

Part 3 · Mount the 60 mm fan and cut the outlet

60 mm fan guard4 × M3 × 35 mm screws, nuts, washersCraft knifeRuler and pencil
  1. 10

    Mark the back wall

    Close the lid and find the SoC heatsink's position from outside. Use the actual fan as a template. The drawing assumes a 57 mm opening, a 67 mm centre height and 50 mm hole spacing; verify these against the fan and closed lid before cutting.

    Check: the circle stays below the hinge.

  2. 11

    Cut, then fit it outside

    Remove the electronics from the cutting area. Cut on a backing board, then make the verified screw holes. Reinforce the cardboard with washers or backing plates. Stack guard, fan and wall from the outside, with the fan's label facing into the box. Screws go in from outside, nuts inside.

    Check: the blades spin freely and the lid still opens.

  3. 12

    Cut the outlet

    Low on the front wall, cut two rows of six 15 mm holes, with centres 15 mm and 35 mm above the floor. One 100 × 20 mm slot works too.

    Check: about 20 cm² open, roughly the size of the fan.

Part 4 · Power the 60 mm fan

USB 5→9 V boost cable, 5.5 × 2.1 mmBarrel-to-screw-terminal adapterScissors or wire strippersSmall screwdriver
  1. 13

    Cut and strip

    Cut the white plug off the fan lead. Strip 5 mm from each wire and twist the strands tight.

    Check: no stray strands.

  2. 14

    Connect to a hub port

    Put red into + and black into − on the screw terminal; tighten and tug. Push the barrel plug into the adapter and the USB end into a hub port. Set the voltage before connecting. This is a 12 V rated fan: 9 V is an optional noise experiment, not a guaranteed starting voltage. Check adapter polarity and repeated starts; use rated 12 V if it stalls. Size the boost cable and powered hub for at least 2.64 W output plus startup margin (about 0.62 A at 5 V at 85% efficiency, before startup).

    Check: the hub has its own supply; no 9 V or 12 V connection goes to the Pi header.

  3. 15

    Check the airflow

    With the hub on, the fan pulls room air in at the back, across the Pi. Hold a hand to the front holes: you should feel air coming out.

    Check: if air goes the wrong way, the fan is backwards. Flip it.
