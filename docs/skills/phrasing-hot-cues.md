# Phrasing, cueing, and hot cues

**Aliases:** phrasing, phrase boundary, 32 beats, downbeat, the one, cue, hot
cue, Safe Launch, Drop Switch, Emergency Exit, Phrase Recovery

## Core idea

Many dance arrangements change in phrase-sized blocks, often 32 beats. Place
cues at meaningful musical changes and launch on matching phrase boundaries so
transitions become repeatable. Count and listen: not every track follows the
same structure.

## Starlight four-cue standard

| Pad | Marker | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 / A | First clean downbeat | Normal mix-in and Safe Launch |
| 2 / B | Main vocal or musical entry | Orientation and quick start |
| 3 / C | Drop, chorus, or peak | Drop Switch or phrase reference |
| 4 / D | Clean outro or final exit phrase | Mix-out warning and Emergency Exit |

Keep the meanings stable even when the exact musical event differs. If a later
controller supplies eight pads, use 5 alternative short mix-in, 6 breakdown, 7
second drop/chorus, and 8 emergency exit or saved loop.

## Cueing workflow

1. Load the incoming track on the inactive deck.
2. Listen through headphones with CUE/PFL.
3. Confirm BPM and beatgrid.
4. Find the first clean downbeat - the `1` of a phrase.
5. Set and audition the cue point.
6. Match tempo.
7. Wait for the correct phrase in the playing track.
8. Launch on the next phrase boundary.

Enable Quantize only after checking the grid. Quantize aligns cues, loops, and
jumps to the beatgrid; it does not fix an incorrect grid or mismatched phrases.

## Moves

- **Safe Launch:** cue Track B, match tempo, wait for a new phrase, launch from Pad 1.
- **Drop Switch:** let Track A reach its final build, then launch Track B from Pad 3 on the next downbeat. Avoid overlapping two vocals or heavy drops.
- **Emergency Exit:** when Track A is running out, jump to Pad 4 and begin the fade to Track B.
- **Phrase Recovery:** at the next phrase boundary, jump the incoming track to the appropriate hot cue. Use sparingly.
