# Fades and EQ handoffs

**Aliases:** fade, blend, long blend, EQ transition, bass handoff, bass swap,
Ghost Exit, Loop-Assisted Fade

Use channel faders for gradual blending and the crossfader for faster handoffs.
The central rule is that both tracks should not remain at full volume with full
bass: this muddies the mix and can clip the output.

## Basic fade

1. Tempo-match and cue Track B.
2. Start Track B on a phrase boundary.
3. Raise its channel fader gradually with its bass reduced.
4. At the next phrase change, lower Track A's bass and introduce Track B's bass.
5. Lower Track A gradually, removing mids/highs as needed.
6. Finish before Track B's main vocal or drop unless the overlap is intentional and auditioned.

## Moves

- **Long Blend:** start B early, fade over 16-32 beats, exchange basslines, fade A out.
- **Bass Handoff:** B enters without bass; on the next `1`, lower A bass and raise B bass.
- **Ghost Exit:** gradually remove A's bass, then mids, then highs.
- **Loop-Assisted Fade:** loop A's last clean phrase, bring B in, perform the EQ handoff, then release or fade A on a phrase boundary.

## Rig-specific practice

- Monitor Track B through the Starlight's headphone cue before the audience hears it.
- Leave the Starlight master stable and control the blend with channel levels/EQ; watch Mixxx meters for clipping.
- Record practice transitions and review them through headphones rather than judging only through the external speaker.
